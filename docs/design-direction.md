### Design Direction: Enterprise-Grade, Production-Quality Prototype

The primary objective is to create a **high-fidelity, enterprise-grade SaaS prototype that closely replicates the actual Sirion platform's visual language, dashboard architecture, and overall user experience.** This should look and feel like a professionally developed enterprise product, not an AI-generated mockup, generic SaaS template, or conceptual wireframe.

#### 1. Reference Fidelity: Replicate Sirion

- Research and study Sirion's actual product interface, dashboards, navigation patterns, visual hierarchy, typography, colour palette, spacing, component styles, and information architecture.
- Use Sirion's official website, product screenshots, demos, and other publicly available product references wherever possible.
- Replicate the actual Sirion interface as closely as possible. Treat Sirion as the primary design benchmark rather than taking creative liberties with the visual identity.
- Maintain consistency with Sirion's existing enterprise design language, including its sidebar, top navigation, dashboard layouts, cards, tables, data visualisations, filters, status indicators, buttons, and modal patterns.
- The final product should look like a natural extension of Sirion's existing platform, as though the feature were developed and released by Sirion itself.

#### 2. Visual Quality and Design Standards

Apply the standards of a senior enterprise product designer working on a commercial SaaS platform.

- **Aesthetic:** Sophisticated, minimal, polished, data-rich, and enterprise-focused.
- **Typography:** Use professional, highly legible typography with a consistent hierarchy across page titles, section headings, labels, metadata, and data values.
- **Colour system:** Follow Sirion's actual brand and interface colours. Use colours consistently for primary actions, secondary actions, backgrounds, borders, alerts, and status indicators.
- **Spacing:** Maintain a precise spacing system, consistent margins, alignment, padding, and component proportions.
- **Components:** Use carefully designed cards, tables, dropdowns, search bars, filters, tabs, tooltips, modals, and navigation elements.
- **Data visualisation:** Where relevant, use clean, information-dense charts with meaningful labels, legends, appropriate scales, and clear visual hierarchy.
- **Layout:** Prioritise structured layouts, efficient use of screen real estate, and clear grouping of related information.

Avoid excessive rounded corners, oversized typography, unnecessary gradients, decorative illustrations, random icons, excessive whitespace, or flashy animations. Do not make the product resemble a consumer-facing application or a generic AI dashboard.

#### 3. Enterprise UX and Interaction Design

Design for the experience of an actual enterprise user who would use this platform in a professional environment.

- Every screen should have a clear information hierarchy and intuitive navigation.
- Ensure that complex information is presented in a digestible, structured manner.
- Use realistic enterprise data, meaningful labels, contextual actions, and relevant metadata.
- Design appropriate loading, empty, success, warning, error, and confirmation states.
- Ensure that buttons, dropdowns, tabs, filters, tables, and other interactive elements behave consistently.
- Maintain continuity between screens so that the product feels like one integrated application rather than a collection of disconnected pages.
- Prioritise usability, clarity, and functional consistency over visual novelty.

#### 4. Implementation and Design Consistency

- Follow the accompanying product specifications strictly for functionality, workflows, features, data fields, and business logic.
- Treat this design brief as the source of truth for the visual experience and the accompanying product document as the source of truth for product requirements.
- Do not omit, simplify, or reinterpret any important functionality specified in the product document.
- Build reusable design components and maintain consistent styling throughout the application.
- Ensure that all screens are designed for a standard desktop enterprise environment, with responsive behaviour where appropriate.
- Use realistic sample data instead of generic placeholder text wherever possible.

#### 5. Quality Bar: What the Final Prototype Must Achieve

The final output must satisfy the following standards:

1. **Visual authenticity:** Someone familiar with Sirion should immediately recognise the interface as belonging to the Sirion ecosystem.
2. **Professional polish:** Every screen should look presentation-ready, with no obvious AI-generated design patterns or amateur UI decisions.
3. **Product coherence:** All screens, workflows, and interactions should feel like parts of the same integrated product.
4. **Functional completeness:** The prototype should demonstrate the specified workflows end-to-end, not merely show static screens.
5. **Attention to detail:** No inconsistent fonts, misaligned elements, awkward spacing, arbitrary colours, broken layouts, generic placeholders, or unfinished components.

**Important:** Do not start designing immediately using a generic template. First understand the product specifications, research Sirion's actual interface, establish a consistent design system based on it, and then build the prototype. Make deliberate design decisions at the level of an experienced enterprise UI/UX designer. The goal is not simply to make the prototype look attractive; it is to make it convincingly resemble a real, production-ready Sirion product.

**One additional instruction I'd strongly recommend:** Ask the AI to prioritise actual Sirion screenshots and product references over its own interpretation of Sirion's brand. Otherwise, it may create something that looks like a generic enterprise SaaS dashboard with Sirion colours rather than something that genuinely resembles Sirion.
